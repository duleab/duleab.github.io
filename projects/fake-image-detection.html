Frequency Domain Analysis

Detecting Invisible Generative Artifacts

Visual inspection often fails against modern generative models like Stable Diffusion, especially at low resolutions. This pipeline uses forensic signals in the frequency and texture domains to identify synthetic media. Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis revealed substantial radial power spectrum divergences at mid-high spatial frequencies — patterns invisible to the human eye but detectable by the model.

Core Finding: Forensic signals such as spectral signatures and Local Binary Pattern (LBP) texture features are statistically far more reliable for deepfake detection than semantic image content, which frequently misleads standard spatial classifiers.

Model Architecture

Two-Phase EfficientNetB0 Transfer Learning

A scratch-trained CNN baseline suffered from overfitting and recall limitations. To address this, a pre-trained EfficientNetB0 model was implemented using a strict two-phase transfer learning strategy — yielding a +17.1 percentage point improvement in recall.

  1. 1

    Phase 1 — Feature Extraction (Frozen Backbone)

    Trained only the classification head, mapping the pre-learned ImageNet feature space to the forensic detection objective. Backbone weights were frozen to preserve low-level texture representations.

  2. 2

    Phase 2 — Fine-Tuning (Unfrozen Top Layers)

    Gradually unfroze the final EfficientNet blocks with a reduced learning rate. This allowed the model to adapt its texture perception specifically to generative artifact patterns without catastrophic forgetting.

Explainability

Grad-CAM Interpretability

For content moderation, trust in the model is essential. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) was implemented to validate that the model focuses on synthetic texture cues and boundaries — rather than memorizing irrelevant dataset biases such as background scenes or lighting conditions.
